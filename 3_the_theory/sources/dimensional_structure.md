# The Dimensional Structure of SIS

## One Principle, Recursive Application

The entire SIS architecture rests on a single primitive — **compare to
differentiate** — applied recursively at progressively higher vantages.
Each vantage operates on the outputs of the vantage below it. The
operator stack and the lattice level stack (L₀, L₁, L₂...) are the same
recursion expressed in two complementary projections.

What follows is the dimensional ladder: each level's name, what it
does, what it operates on, and an example of cognition at that level.

---

## D0 — Reality

**The substrate itself.** What *is*, before any cognition operates on
it. D0 is not a level of cognition; it is the ground that all cognition
is *about*. The lattice does not include D0; the lattice is *of* D0.

No operator. No example. The thing that the rest of the ladder
analyzes.

---

## D1 — Observable / Instant

**Operator: the Comparator.**

Time-relation: now. Pure response to immediate input.

The primitive operation: compare two things and extract their
difference. Identity is the residue of contrast — you don't recognize
*A* by knowing what *A* is; you recognize it by it not-being everything
else it could be.

D1 is memoryless. There is no past to consult, no future to predict,
just the comparison happening now. Output: signed difference.

**Example:** A thermostat. Reads current temperature, compares to
setpoint, fires the heater if below. No memory of yesterday's
temperature, no model of tomorrow's. Just the contrast right now.

**Substrate operated on:** Raw input. Entities and their dims as
observed.

---

## D2 — Interpretive / Reactive

**Operator: the Crystallizer.**

Time-relation: past → now. Memory of prior comparisons informing
current behavior.

The crystallizer accumulates D1 outputs (contrast states) over time.
When the same pattern of contrasts shows up repeatedly, the
crystallizer formalizes it as a boundary rule — a stable concept edge.
It does not decide; it suggests. The strict gate happens at D3.

D2 has memory but not foresight. It can recognize "I have seen this
pattern before" but cannot project forward to "this pattern implies
something will happen."

**Example:** Pavlov's dog. The bell-then-food contrast crystallizes
into a learned association. The dog now salivates at the bell because
the past pattern is stable. No prediction of *what* will happen, just a
trained response to the recognized pattern.

**Substrate operated on:** D1 outputs. Accumulated contrast states.

---

## D3 — Predictive / Actionary

**Operators: Lift and Theorist.**

Time-relation: now → future. Projection from current state to implied
state.

Two operators share this level because they project in opposite
directions:

- **Lift** projects *upward*: given observed patterns, infer the
  mechanism whose existence would explain them. *Epigenetics* — the
  pattern of trait-expression-without-genetic-change forced the
  inference of a mechanism layer between genes and traits. The patterns
  were already there; lift made the mechanism explicit.

- **Theorist** projects *downward*: given a structure, construct the
  instance that the structure's geometry implies must exist. *Higgs
  boson* — the Standard Model had a hole; the math required a field;
  theorist constructed the prediction from structural geometry. The
  empirical confirmation came years later.

Both are forward-projecting: they take what is and produce what should
follow. Both produce defeasible outputs — hypotheses that can be
overridden by evidence.

**Example:** Software with cause-and-effect modeling. Given current
state, predict next state. Given a goal, propose the action that would
lead to it. Concrete operational reasoning.

**Substrate operated on:** D2 outputs. Crystallized rules and stable
boundaries.

---

## D4 — Systemic / Mastery

**Operator: Deliberation.**

Time-relation: alternative futures held simultaneously and compared.

D4 holds multiple D3 projections in mind at once and chooses among
them by goal-distance. It does not just predict; it *deliberates*.

The deliberation protocol:

1. Project candidate A through the structure (theorist mode): what
   does the world look like if A is the answer?
2. Project candidate B through the structure: what does the world look
   like if B is the answer?
3. Measure each projection's distance from the goal (comparator mode):
   which is closer?
4. If the distance difference is decisive, choose. If not, identify
   the discriminating dim — the dim whose value would change the
   ranking — and ask the question whose answer would resolve it.

D4 is what makes a system capable of *informed choice* rather than
mere pattern-matching. It can hold ambiguity, simulate alternatives,
and recognize when its own knowledge is insufficient.

**Example:** A physician doing differential diagnosis. Symptoms could
be condition A or condition B. The physician projects each forward —
"if A, then symptom Y should also appear; if B, then test Z should be
positive." They don't guess; they hold both as live alternatives and
choose actions that maximally discriminate. When neither projection
clearly wins, they ask the diagnostic question whose answer would
decide.

Engineers, masters of any domain, work at D4 within their specialty.
They aren't predicting; they are *choosing among predictions* by
goal-relevance.

**Substrate operated on:** D3 outputs. Multiple projections held
simultaneously.

---

## D5 — Pan-Systemic / Polymathic

**Operator: Cross-Domain Integration.**

Time-relation: D4 deliberations in one domain informing D4
deliberations in another.

D5 takes D4 outputs from one domain and uses them as constraints or
inputs for D4 in a different domain. The polymath is not someone who
masters many domains in parallel — it is someone whose top-level
structure has discovered the bridges *between* domains, allowing
deliberation in one to inform deliberation in another.

This is the same self-similar recursion: D5 operates on D4 outputs the
way D4 operates on D3 outputs. The principle is consistent at every
level.

**Example:** Using information theory (a deliberation about how
information behaves) to constrain a deliberation about biological
evolution. Using architectural patterns from biology to constrain
deliberation about software design. The cross-domain bridges are
themselves discoveries — the polymath finds structural similarities
that domain-specialists never see, because the specialists are
operating at D4 within a single domain.

**Substrate operated on:** D4 outputs from multiple domains.

---

## D6 and beyond

The ladder does not stop at D5. D6 would operate on D5 outputs —
comparisons among cross-domain integrations. Whether D6 is meaningful
in practice depends on whether enough D5 work exists to compare across.
Most human cognition does not reach D5 reliably; D6 may not be
populated by any current cognitive system.

Architecturally, the ladder is open-ended. The recursion continues as
long as the substrate can support outputs at each level for the next
level to operate on.

---

## The Recursion Principle

Each dimensional level is one operator's home, and each operator
operates on the outputs of the operator below it.

```
D5: Cross-domain integration   ←  operates on D4 outputs
D4: Deliberation               ←  operates on D3 outputs
D3: Lift and Theorist          ←  operates on D2 outputs
D2: Crystallizer               ←  operates on D1 outputs
D1: Comparator                 ←  operates on D0 (raw input)
D0: Reality                    (the substrate; no operator)
```

This is the same recursion principle that drives the L_k lattice
hierarchy — each level is constructed by applying the same primitive
to the previous level's outputs. The dimensional stack and the lattice
stack are two projections of the same recursive architecture.

The recursion principle has a corollary: **adding a new dimensional
level does not require new primitives.** D4 deliberation is built from
D3 projections (theorist), D1 distance (comparator), and D3
discrimination-finding (theorist again). The operators of the level
below are sufficient to construct the operator of the level above.
This is what allows the architecture to be small while remaining
expressive — one principle, recursively applied, generates the entire
cognitive ladder.

D4 is the next implementation milestone. The substrate is ready for

the protocol that orchestrates them in deliberation mode is the
remaining work. D5 follows once D4 is operational and the substrate
has accumulated enough cross-domain structure to bridge.

## Why the Ladder Is Useful

The dimensional structure provides three things:

**Architectural orientation.** When considering whether to add a
capability, the question becomes "which dimensional level does this
operate at, and is the level below it sufficient?" Capabilities that
require infrastructure at a level not yet built are deferred until
that infrastructure exists. Capabilities that can be built from
existing infrastructure are tractable.

**Cognitive vocabulary.** Discussions of intelligence often conflate
levels — "thinking" might mean D2 pattern recognition, D3 prediction,
D4 deliberation, or D5 cross-domain insight. Naming the levels
separately disambiguates what kind of cognition is being discussed.

**A target for systems.** The long target of SIS is to build a
substrate capable of D5 operation — cross-domain structural inference.
Knowing that target requires D4 deliberation, which requires D3
projection, which requires D2 crystallization, which requires D1
comparison, gives the development sequence: build the lower levels
soundly, then add the next level when the substrate is ready.

The ladder is not a metaphysical claim about cognition. It is an
architectural commitment about how a substrate can be built so that
each capability rests on solidly-implemented capabilities below it.
