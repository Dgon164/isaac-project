# Methods Write-Up: The Ternary Activation Experiment
**Interleave World program — June 11, 2026. Companion to tern_test.py and the session record.**

## 1. Origin and hypothesis

The experiment operationalizes the researcher's excitation/inhibition theory at its maximum. The theory, validated earlier in the program on the gateless net, holds that a unit's contribution decomposes into sign (excite vs inhibit) and magnitude (volume), with low magnitude meaning "abstain." The maximal version asks: if sign carries the semantics, can magnitude be deleted entirely? The test instrument is an activation function that outputs only three values — +1 (excite), 0 (abstain), −1 (inhibit) — replacing the FFN nonlinearity of a standard transformer block.

Three predictions were registered before any code ran, anchored to the program's then-current theory that gate hardness trades structure for speed: (P1) the ternary net learns the fixed-lag components but more slowly than ReLU, because quantization adds gradient noise; (P2) the cycle component degrades relative to ReLU at matched budget; (P3, registered with explicit low confidence) orbit generalization lands near ReLU's, since ternary is the hardest possible gate and hard gating was believed to follow the memorizing trajectory.

## 2. The activation and its gradient

Forward pass, applied elementwise to the FFN's 256 hidden units:

```
f(x) = +1  if x >  0.5
        0  if −0.5 ≤ x ≤ 0.5
       −1  if x < −0.5
```

The dead zone (threshold 0.5) implements "abstain"; it was fixed, not tuned. Because f has zero derivative almost everywhere, it cannot be trained by ordinary backpropagation. The standard remedy is the straight-through estimator (STE; Bengio 2013, as used in BinaryNet, Courbariaux & Bengio 2016): the backward pass pretends the function was approximately identity, passing the incoming gradient through unchanged wherever |x| ≤ 1.5 and zeroing it outside that window. The window prevents gradients from pushing units that are already deep in saturation. Implementation is a custom `torch.autograd.Function` (`TernarySTE` in variants.py): forward quantizes, backward multiplies the gradient by the mask `(|x| ≤ 1.5)`. Both thresholds (0.5 dead zone, 1.5 STE window) are conventional defaults; neither was swept, which is a known limitation noted in §7.

## 3. Architecture and what was held fixed

The net is the program's standard 2-layer harness (variants.py `Net`/`Block`): pre-norm transformer, D=64, 4 heads of dim 16, FFN width 256, learned positional embeddings over 49 positions, untied Linear(64→22) output head, causal mask, dropout 0. The only change from the ReLU baseline is the FFN activation; attention remains softmax. This isolates the activation as the single manipulated variable. Everything else — tokenization, vocabulary, sequence layout, optimizer family — is identical to every prior variant in the program, so all earlier numbers (relu, gelu, softmax-FFN, leaky, gateless) are valid comparison points.

## 4. Corpora

Two corpora, both generated by the sealed-protocol world generator (generator.py), were reused unchanged from earlier experiments so that results are directly comparable.

**Standard corpus** (speed and component measurements): 2,000 training sequences, 200 sealed test sequences drawn exclusively from three held-out (d, period, mode) parameter cells, generator seed 11, custody SHA-256 recorded before any training in CUSTODY.txt.

**Orbit corpus** (the structure/memorization discriminator): regenerated with RNG seed 77 by the same construction as the original orbit experiment. Training set of 2,000 sequences excludes every key where d=2 co-occurs with an odd step seed; test sets are 150 sequences of d=2 with odd seeds (the unseen orbit — digit transitions never observed as pairs in training) and 150 with even seeds (seen-orbit control). A net that memorized (prev, current)→next digit pairs fails the odd-seed set; a net that learned the additive rule transfers.

## 5. Training procedure

Identical recipe to all 2-layer comparisons in the program: AdamW, learning rate 3e-3, weight decay 0.01, cosine annealing over the run length, batch 64, gradient clipping at norm 1.0, torch.manual_seed(0), CPU. Three training runs were performed: standard corpus for 15 epochs (speed comparison point, matching the original variant suite budget), standard corpus for 45 epochs (convergence point, matching the time-vs-depth study), and orbit corpus for 45 epochs (convergence, matching the leaky/orbit study). Wall time forced a mechanical adaptation: the single-CPU sandbox could not fit all three runs in one command window, so tern_test.py was staged into three invocations (`a`, `b`, `c`), one per run, with no change to any training logic.

## 6. Measurements

**Per-component accuracy** (standard corpus, sealed cells): next-token accuracy at CYCLE, STEP, and FIB slots restricted to rounds 8–15, where the world is fully deterministic given the prefix and accuracy is therefore a pure competence measure rather than a mixture with irreducible early-round entropy.

**Speed** is read as which components are solved at the 15-epoch checkpoint, against the ReLU baseline's known 15-epoch profile.

**Orbit transfer**: STEP accuracy, rounds 8–15, on the unseen-orbit and seen-orbit test sets, against the converged 45-epoch references measured the previous turn under the same seed and recipe: relu 0.998/0.225 (seen/unseen), leaky α=0.1 1.000/0.257, gateless 0.998/0.522.

## 7. Results and status

At 15 epochs: STEP 1.000, FIB 1.000 — full gated-class learning speed; P1's "slower" component falsified. At 45 epochs: CYC 0.664, the highest 2-layer cycle score in the program (gateless 2L at the same budget: 0.381) — P2 falsified in the opposite direction; this number is single-seed and is flagged in the record as non-load-bearing until replicated. Orbit at convergence: seen 1.000, unseen 0.482 — near-gateless transfer from the hardest gate in the suite; P3 falsified.

The theory revision recorded: structure-vs-speed is not monotone in gate hardness. The account that currently fits all variants is activation channel capacity — ReLU transmits full magnitudes (sufficient bandwidth to support pair-lookup memorization), ternary transmits ~1.58 bits per feature (too narrow to memorize through) while retaining a strong nonlinearity's trainability. This is an information-bottleneck framing and simultaneously the excite/inhibit theory at its maximum: sign is the semantics; magnitude proved negotiable.

Known limitations, all explicit in the record: single seed throughout; thresholds 0.5/1.5 untuned; one world; 2 layers only; the channel-capacity account is a posit fit to five activation variants, not yet independently tested (a direct test would dial capacity continuously, e.g. k-level quantization for k ∈ {2, 3, 5, 9, ∞}, predicting transfer falls as k rises).

## 8. Reproduction

Files: variants.py (TernarySTE class and the `tern` branch in Block.ffn), tern_test.py (all three stages; run `python3 tern_test.py a`, then `b`, then `c`), generator.py (world), train.jsonl / test_SEALED.jsonl / CUSTODY.txt (standard corpus and custody). Seeds: torch 0 for all training; orbit corpus RNG 77; standard corpus generator seed 11. Hardware-independent: results above were produced on a single CPU core; any machine reproduces them with the same seeds, and the per-run wall time on a modern GPU is seconds.
