up):

- **Waves:** door-count sweep 50 → 5,000 (the D² correlation + permutation
  nulls are the first wall; prediction: knee near D≈2–3k on this machine);
  stream-length × concurrency capacity grid (does 8 waves work at T=10k?).
- **discover():** feature-count sweep (atom library grows ~d²·grid;
  prediction: candidate-matrix knee near d≈60–100, fix = mine candidates
  from the correlation top-k instead of materializing all).
- **Exemplars:** covtype latency/accuracy vs exemplar count 10k→406k;
  MNIST 70k (curriculum 1.2 — also the topology-doors rematch at 28×28,
  prediction: hole doors rise from ignored to elected).

  slowly changes; prediction: per-item cost stays flat, concept count
  follows a Heaps-law curve, drift shows up as new-concept rate — which
  doubles as the drift detector tier 4.2 lacks.
- Then and only then: the two architectural fixes (doors-as-inverted-index,
  lattice-as-cascade) applied where knees actually appeared.

## 3. Forgetting (reframed: never deletion — demotion)

"Everything enters, nothing leaves" stands. Forgetting is about what you
COMPARE against, not what you keep:

- **Comparison-forgetting is already built** (condensation: per-item cost
  flat at ~2k while raw memory grows). Extend with tiered memory: hot
  exemplars (recent/high-use/anomalous) vs cold archive (recallable, not
  searched).
- **The principled criterion — forget what you understand:** an instance
  may be archived when its concept reconstructs it within noise; its
  information now lives in the closure. Memory cost migrates from data to
  law as understanding grows. Corollary: ANOMALIES ARE NEVER ARCHIVED —
  the residual is the precious part.
- **No catastrophic forgetting, by construction:** no weights → learning B
  cannot overwrite A. Cheap to demonstrate (learn laws in domain A, then
  B, re-test A) and worth demonstrating loudly — it's a structural
  advantage over nets, free of charge.
- Door-level hygiene: wave groups/compounds that stop earning (support
  decays, null margin gone) get demoted from the index per iceberg
  pruning — again index removal, not data removal.

## 4. Extraction efficiency (cheaper discovery per law)

- **Law-library warm starts (learning to learn, curriculum 2.5):** seed the
  frontier with term shapes from previously discovered laws. Prediction:
  the 11th product-family law needs measurably fewer samples/rounds than
  the 1st — the first quantitative "experience makes discovery cheaper"
  result. If flat, that's the honest null.
- **Spectral proposals:** FFT pre-pass proposes frequencies in O(n log n)
  instead of grid×refine — kills the k≈9 reach limit found by red-team A4.
- **Sequential permutation tests:** stop each null early at clear
  pass/fail — est. 5–10× cheaper gates, same guarantees.
- **Dossier mining as candidate generation** (see migration loop): let
  precedents propose compounds instead of exhaustive bin-pair search.
